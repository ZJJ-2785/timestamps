### Install

**Windows:** from 1.1.0, use Settings → About → **Update**, or run the setup. Your data is kept either way. The setup isn't code-signed, so SmartScreen may show "Windows protected your PC": click **More info** → **Run anyway**.

**macOS:** unzip, drag **Timestamps** into *Applications* and open it from there. It isn't notarized by Apple, so the first start is blocked: open it once, then *System Settings → Privacy & Security* → **Open Anyway** (on macOS 14 and earlier, Control-click the app → *Open*). It asks for Accessibility (window titles and documents), Screen Recording (screenshots) and, per browser, Automation (website addresses). The macOS version hasn't been tested on many Macs yet, so please report what doesn't work.

### What's new

- **Statistics**
  - The totals show the change from the period before: this week so far against the same days last week, and so on. Hover for the dates and the earlier total.
  - **Focus**: work sessions, average and longest session (click it to open that day), application switches per hour, and how long your sessions are.
  - **Rhythm**: the average day of the week (or, for a shorter range, the hours of the day), peak hour, busiest day and weekend share.
  - **Top applications** over the range, by day, week or month.
- **Day view layout** (by @Dan-k391, #2)
  - Resizing follows the pointer and catches at thirds and halves; blocks have a minimum size.
  - Dropping a block on another swaps the two.
  - The timeline has its own height, and its lanes fill it.
  - Screenshot thumbnails fill their card.
  - A pin locks the layout against dragging, resizing and reset.
- **Sidebar**: collapsing and expanding animate. Collapsed, it's a rail of icons with names on hover. The stopwatch and countdown are icons there too, and a running one shows its time with its actions in a menu.
- **Animations**: charts and bars grow in, cards rise in, tabs slide to the new choice, a new theme cross-fades, and panels slide out. They follow your system's reduce-motion setting.
- **dkhd theme**: its light surfaces are tinted with the theme's pink.
- **Reliability**
  - Started from inside another app's container, Windows would hide Timestamps' data in that app's folder and close Timestamps whenever that app restarts. Timestamps now says so and doesn't start.
  - The setup starts the app through Explorer, so an install or update can't end up inside such a container.
  - A crash now writes its cause to `timestamps.log` instead of vanishing.
